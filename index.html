Side project · live demo

about the black hole.

A real-time raymarcher written to render gravitational lensing from first principles — no library, no asset, no shortcut. I built it to understand how light actually behaves around mass. This is it, running live:

Rendering live — fps

A.Problem

Render a convincing black hole — not a shader-toy approximation, but something where every feature depends on light actually bending. If the image looks right, the math is right.

B.Challenge

Lensing can't be a screen-space warp. Photons follow curved paths, so the far side of the disk must lens up over the shadow and self-occlude — that forces a per-pixel raymarcher integrating a curved path.

C.Architecture

One fullscreen GLSL fragment shader, no geometry. Each pixel reconstructs a world ray, marches it while bending toward the singularity, accumulates disk emission on plane crossings, and samples a procedural starfield in the deflected direction.

D.Performance

Held to 60 fps by an adaptive step size (fine near the horizon, coarse far out), early-out when a ray escapes, a hard 240-step ceiling, transmittance termination, and a device-pixel-ratio cap.

E.Decisions

Newtonian-style deflection over a full Schwarzschild geodesic — visually indistinguishable at this scale, several times cheaper. Disk emission is integrated in the same pass, the only way to lens and self-occlude it correctly.

F.What's next

Swap in the true Kerr geodesic integrator (drafted in src/shaders/kerr.glsl), a free-orbit camera, and a fullscreen dive where you cross the event horizon.
