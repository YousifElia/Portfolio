Real-time Black Hole

A gravitationally-lensed black hole raymarched in a single fragment shader — no engine, no libraries, no textures. Every pixel traces its own bent light path. Hover to wake it, then take the dive.

60 fps on an iGPU·1 draw call·~270 lines GLSL·0 KB textures
GLSL WebGL TypeScript
source ↗
rendering live — hover to wake

A.Problem

Render a black hole where every feature falls out of light actually bending — if it looks right, the math is right.

B.Challenge

Lensing can't be a screen-space warp — the disk's far side must lens up over the shadow. That forces per-pixel raymarching.

C.Architecture

One fullscreen fragment shader, no geometry: each pixel marches a ray, bends it toward the singularity, accumulates disk light on plane crossings.

D.Performance

60 fps on an iGPU via adaptive step size, early ray escape, a hard step ceiling, and a device-pixel-ratio cap.

E.Decisions

Newtonian-style deflection over full geodesics — visually identical at this scale, several times cheaper per frame.

F.What's next

True Kerr geodesics, a free-orbit camera, and a horizon-crossing dive sequence.
